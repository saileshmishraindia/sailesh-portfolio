Semiconductor School

# Semiconductor School

This is a learning environment for students who want more than a formula list. We begin with the physical idea, build the model, test it in simulation, and then connect it to the electronics and VLSI decisions that matter later.

Start the learning loop Return to My Foundation
Meaning

Why are we learning this?

Semiconductor School is not a place where formulas appear without context. Every chapter begins with the same question: what is physically happening, why does it matter, and where will this idea show up again in electronics and VLSI?

PILLAR 01

Physical intuition before math

Before we write the equation, we look at the device, the carriers, the field, the energy levels, and the circuit conditions that create the effect.

  • What is changing?
  • Why does it change?
  • What does the device “see”?
PILLAR 02

Meaning and purpose

Every concept connects to a real engineering problem: switching, current flow, biasing, charge storage, threshold behavior, noise, gain, or speed.

  • What problem does it describe?
  • Why does it exist?
  • When will I use it later?
PILLAR 03

Simulation as a guide

Once the model is formed, simulation becomes a way to check assumptions, visualize trends, and ask better questions: what happens when the bias changes, the voltage changes, or the geometry changes?

  • Observe the waveform
  • Compare with the model
  • Learn the reason behind the result
PILLAR 04

Connect it to VLSI

Semiconductor physics is not a disconnected subject. It is the reason a transistor turns on, a cell switches, a circuit meets timing, and a chip behaves the way it does.

  • device physics → circuit behavior
  • CMOS → logic and timing
  • VLSI → design decisions
Practice

Learn by understanding, not by memorizing

Practice here is designed to build engineering judgment. We do not stop at “what is the formula?” We ask: why does the answer make sense, what is physically happening, and how would this appear in a real circuit or device?

The progression

Understand → Calculate → Predict → Simulate → Explain → Design. The goal is not only to get the right number. The goal is to understand why the number behaves that way and where it fits in an actual circuit.

Level 1 — Understand

Simple conceptual questions about the device, the field, the carrier motion, or the operating region.

Level 2 — Apply

Use the model, estimate the result, and check the assumptions behind it.

Level 3 — Analyze

Reason about a transistor, diode, junction, or circuit under changing conditions.

Level 4 — Simulate

Verify the behavior in LTspice or a similar tool and compare it with the expectation.

Level 5 — Engineer

Make a design choice, justify it, and explain the trade-off in terms of physics and circuit behavior.

What lies underneath

Not an archive. A way of working.

The material behind this page spans semiconductor and circuit notes, repeated LTSpice experiments, annotated class material, and structured interview preparation. Together they show four practices that continue into the rest of the portfolio.

PILLAR 01

Fundamentals before abstraction

Semiconductor physics, PN junctions, BJT and MOSFET operation, RC behavior, CMOS fabrication, inverter transfer characteristics, and digital-system basics form the vocabulary used to reason about larger designs.

  • Device behavior
  • Analog and digital foundations
  • CMOS and VLSI design flow
PILLAR 02

Simulation as a thinking tool

The LTSpice work moves from passive circuits and device characterization into amplifiers, oscillators, data-converter blocks, power supplies, protection circuits, RF stages, and application-level models.

  • Bias, transient, AC, and frequency response
  • Iterative schematic refinement
  • Behavior checked before conclusions
PILLAR 03

Learning through explanation

Numbered lessons, annotated notes, recap material, and versions prepared for different levels show that understanding was repeatedly reorganized for teaching, not only collected for personal reference.

  • Progressive lesson structure
  • Annotated technical notes
  • Audience-aware explanations
PILLAR 04

Preparation through technical dialogue

Placement discussions, doubt-resolution material, and cohort-based mock-interview preparation add a different test: can a concept be recalled, defended, and communicated under questioning?

  • Concept-first interview preparation
  • Reasoning under constraints
  • Feedback and revision
From source material to practice

Three bodies of work, one engineering habit

Each body of material contributes something different. The value is in their relationship, not in publishing every file.

The recurring pattern

A concept is studied at device or circuit level, exercised through a model, refined by inspecting behavior, and then restated clearly enough to teach or defend. That same loop supports the portfolio's analog design, digital design, VLSI, CAD/EDA, and systems work.

LTSpice experiments

Verified themes include RC networks, device characterization, BJT and op-amp stages, oscillators, buck/boost and flyback conversion, protection circuits, delta-sigma and sample-and-hold blocks, and staged RF front-end work.

Annotated VLSI classes

The notes progress from introductory VLSI and Verilog through semiconductor physics, RC analysis, CMOS devices, inverter behavior, amplifiers, netlists, simulation flow, and design flow, with recap and audience-specific variants.

Mock-interview practice

Recurring cohort materials indicate a structured preparation process connected to technical mentoring. Individual evaluations and named records remain private and are not linked here.
